## 1. Executive summary — sector view: **CONSTRUCTIVE, but late-cycle on asset quality and mid-cycle on margins; be selective**

The Indian banking system in mid-2026 is in the **strongest structural health in over a decade** — but that
strength is now *the consensus*, largely reflected in price, and two cyclical pressures are building.

| Dimension | Reading | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| **Asset quality** | GNPA **1.8%**, NNPA **0.4%** (Mar-2026) — multi-decadal low (from 11.2% peak, FY18) | Bottoming; only way is up (benignly, to ~2.0–2.1% FY27) |
| **Credit growth** | **10.4–11.7%** — moderated from the ~16% post-COVID surge | Decelerating, then stabilising |
| **Margins (NIM)** | Private **3.87%**, PSU **2.71%** — compressed by the rate-cut cycle | Under pressure; near a trough |
| **Deposit mobilisation** | The binding constraint; CD ratios elevated; FCNR(B) inflows a relief | Tight |
| **Capital & profitability** | High CAR, sustained RoA; sector RoEs 11–16% | Robust |
| **Rates (repo)** | **5.25%**, neutral stance (June-2026 MPC), from a 6.5% peak | At/near cycle bottom |

**The one-line thesis:** you are buying a clean, well-capitalised system at the *top* of the asset-quality
cycle and the *bottom* of the margin cycle. The earnings tailwind now must come from **credit-growth
revival and NIM stabilisation**, not from further provision write-backs. Valuation dispersion is wide —
that is where the alpha is, not in the sector call.

## 2. Macro backdrop (top-down)

The bank is a **levered play on the economy and the rate cycle**. Five macro vectors matter:

**2.1 Growth.** RBI projects FY27 real GDP at **6.6%** (cut from 6.9% at the June-2026 MPC). India remains
the fastest-growing large economy, but the downgrade signals softer momentum — below-normal monsoon risk to
rural demand, elevated energy prices, and global uncertainty. Bank credit growth historically runs at
~1.5–2.0× nominal GDP; at ~10.5% nominal GDP that supports the ~11% system credit-growth estimate.

**2.2 Inflation.** CPI forecast **raised to 5.1%** for FY27 (from 4.6%), driven by partial pass-through of
energy prices to pumps, higher input costs, and a weak-monsoon risk premium. Rising inflation *caps the
RBI's room to cut further* — important, because banks' near-term NIM relief depends on deposit costs
falling faster than asset yields, which a hawkish tilt slows.

**2.3 Rates & RBI policy.** Repo held at **5.25%**, **neutral** stance. The cutting cycle (from a 6.5%
peak) is the direct cause of current NIM compression: floating-rate assets (EBLR-linked retail/MSME loans)
reprice down *immediately*, while term deposits reprice with a lag. So the rate cut hurt banks first and
will help later as deposit costs catch up. **We are near the rate-cycle bottom** — the point of maximum NIM
pain, historically a favourable entry window for bank margins over the following 12–18 months.

**2.4 External / INR.** RBI flagged **rupee pressure** and announced measures to **attract capital flows**
(including FCNR(B) deposit incentives, ~US$50bn of inflows adding ~1.8% to system deposits). A weak INR is
double-edged for banks: it pressures imported inflation (hawkish for rates) but the FCNR route directly
eases the deposit crunch. Watch crude — India's current-account and INR are oil-sensitive.

**2.5 Liquidity.** System liquidity and the loan-to-deposit (CD) ratio are the sector's real constraint
today: credit outgrew deposits for two years, pushing CD ratios up and forcing banks into a **deposit war**
that raised funding costs. Deposit mobilisation — not loan demand — is the binding scarce resource.

## 3. RBI & Government — the regulatory and policy lens

Banking is the most heavily regulated sector; policy *is* a fundamental.

- **Prudential strength by design.** The post-AQR (2015) clean-up, IBC/bankruptcy code, PCA framework, and
  higher provisioning turned the sector from fragility to robustness. GNPA at 1.8% is the payoff.
- **Priority Sector Lending (PSL)** — 40% of ANBC — remains a structural drag on blended yields and a
  compliance cost; the PSLC market monetises the gap.
- **PSU banks:** the government's stance has shifted from *recapitalise-and-hold* to *value-harvest* —
  continued (slow) privatisation intent, better governance, and PSU RoEs now competitive (SBI RoE ~16%).
  Bank of Baroda trades **below book (0.82×)** — the PSU discount is the sector's clearest value/risk debate.
- **Financial inclusion & DPI:** Jan Dhan–Aadhaar–Mobile (JAM), UPI, and account aggregators have
  formalised credit and lowered acquisition costs — a genuine structural tailwind (§5).
- **Consumer protection / unsecured lending:** the RBI raised risk weights on unsecured retail and NBFC
  exposures in 2023–24 to cool an overheating segment — a live governor on retail-credit growth.

## 4. Sector fundamentals — where we are in the credit cycle

**4.1 Asset quality (late-cycle — the good news is priced).** GNPA **1.8%**, NNPA **0.4%** (Mar-2026), the
best in a generation, with high Provision Coverage. The honest read: *this is a cyclical peak in quality.*
Credit costs cannot fall much further; incremental earnings can no longer lean on provision write-backs.
The forward risk is **normalisation** — RBI itself guides GNPA back to 2.0–2.1% in FY27. **Watch unsecured
retail and microfinance** — the pockets where the next slippage cycle typically begins.

**4.2 Margins (mid-cycle, near trough).** NIMs compressed — private **3.87%** (−15bps QoQ), PSU **2.71%**
(−10bps) — as the rate cut repriced assets faster than deposits. This is cyclical and reverses as deposit
costs catch up; NIM stabilisation is the single biggest FY27 earnings swing factor.

**4.3 Growth & deposits.** Credit growth **10.4–11.7%**, moderating and healthier (less unsecured froth).
The constraint is deposits: elevated CD ratios and a deposit war. **CASA mix** is the key differentiator —
banks with sticky low-cost current/savings deposits (HDFC, ICICI, SBI, Kotak) defend margins far better
than those funding growth with bulk term deposits.

**4.4 Capital & profitability.** Sector is well-capitalised (CAR comfortably above regulatory minima), and
RoAs are healthy. RoE dispersion (§7) is wide: **16% (SBI/ICICI) to <4% (IDFC First, in investment phase)**.

**Base rate to remember:** Indian banking is deeply cyclical. GNPA went 2% → 11% → 2% in a decade. Any
thesis that extrapolates today's 1.8% GNPA and low credit costs *forever* is underwriting the wrong part of
the cycle.

## 5. Structural / secular themes (the multi-year tailwinds)

1. **Financialisation & formalisation.** Household savings shifting into formal credit and financial assets;
   GST/UPI formalising MSME cash-flows into lendable, data-rich borrowers.
2. **Retail & MSME credit deepening** — India's credit-to-GDP (~55–60%) is low vs peers; long runway, but
   the unsecured slice needs discipline (RBI risk-weight actions).
3. **Digital rails (UPI).** India already *won* retail payments with UPI — a public good that lowered CASA
   acquisition cost and fee friction. This is the crucial backdrop for the CBDC discussion in §6.
4. **Fee & cross-sell income** — wealth, insurance, cards — a re-rating lever for franchise banks less
   dependent on spread income.

## 6. Blockchain, CBDC & the disruption question (requested deep-dive)

This is where hype and reality diverge most, so treat it analytically.

**6.1 What the Digital Rupee (e₹) actually is.** The e₹ is a **central bank digital currency** — a sovereign
liability of the RBI, issued on distributed-ledger/blockchain infrastructure but **centralised and
RBI-controlled**. That is the opposite of decentralised crypto (Bitcoin/stablecoins). RBI runs a
**"double stance"**: it embraces the *technology* (DLT) for the CBDC while remaining cautious-to-restrictive
on private crypto.

**6.2 Adoption reality — small, and shrinking.** Retail e₹ in circulation **fell to ₹7.71bn** (from
₹10.16bn a year earlier) — a *rounding error* next to UPI's tens of trillions of rupees in monthly volume.
The blunt truth: **UPI already solved India's retail-payments problem**, so the e₹'s retail value
proposition is weak. Pilot only, closed user groups, P2P/P2M.

**6.3 Where blockchain actually threatens or helps banks:**
- **Disintermediation risk (the real long-tail threat).** If a retail CBDC ever scaled with no holding
  limits, households could hold central-bank money directly, **draining low-cost bank deposits** — the
  cheapest funding banks have. This is the structural risk worth monitoring. RBI's deliberately slow,
  limit-bound rollout is *designed* to avoid exactly this. Near-term impact: **negligible.**
- **Wholesale CBDC & tokenisation (the real opportunity).** The action is in **wholesale CBDC for interbank
  settlement**, tokenised deposits, and tokenised government securities — faster, atomic, cheaper
  settlement. This *helps* efficient banks. **BRICS CBDC linkage** (RBI proposal for the 2026 India-hosted
  summit) points to cross-border settlement rails that could bypass correspondent-banking friction.
- **DLT in trade finance, KYC, bond issuance** — pilots exist, but none are yet material to earnings.

**6.4 Verdict on blockchain vs banking.** For the next 3–5 years, blockchain/CBDC is **neither a near-term
disruptor nor an earnings driver** for Indian banks — it is a *watch item*. The asymmetric risk is a
future retail-CBDC that erodes deposit franchises; the asymmetric opportunity is wholesale
tokenisation/settlement for the strongest, most tech-forward banks. **Banks that own low-cost deposit
franchises and invest in tokenised-settlement capability are structurally best placed either way.** Anyone
pitching "blockchain will disrupt Indian banks soon" is ignoring that UPI already occupied the retail
ground and that the CBDC is, by RBI design, a controlled complement — not a competitor.

## 7. Micro — comparative valuation & quality (live screener data, Jul-2026)

Banks are valued on **Price/Book vs RoE**, not P/E. The market pays up for franchise quality (CASA, low
credit cost, consistent RoE); it discounts PSU governance/cyclicality.

**Cross-sectional signal:** the market is paying **2.8×** book for Kotak's 11% RoE but **1.7×** for SBI's
16% and **0.82×** for BoB's 14% — a governance/consistency premium worth scrutinising. On a pure
RoE-for-multiple basis, **SBI and BoB screen cheap; Kotak and AU screen expensive**. That is a valuation
observation, not a recommendation — the premium may be justified by durability, which is exactly what deeper
work (deposit franchise, credit-cost through-cycle) must test.

## 8. How this maps to *our* firm's universe (₹300–30,000cr)

Most names above are large-caps, **outside** our micro/small/mid mandate. The in-universe banking
opportunity set is narrower and higher-variance:
- **Small Finance Banks** (AU has outgrown the band; Equitas, Ujjivan, Suryoday, ESAF sit in/near it) —
  higher NIM, higher microfinance/asset-quality risk.
- **Old private banks** (Karur Vysya, City Union, DCB, CSB, Karnataka Bank, South Indian Bank) — regional
  franchises, cheaper, governance and growth variance.
- These are where a **multibagger** can exist (re-rating from a cleaned-up balance sheet or a franchise
  inflection) — and where the **forensic lens matters most**.

**Forensic note (ADR-0002):** for lenders the firm **suppresses Beneish M-score / Piotroski / Sloan
accruals** — they assume a non-financial accrual structure banks don't have. The bank forensic screen uses
**GNPA drift, provision-coverage (PCR), restructured/SMA book, slippage ratio, CET1 adequacy, and
promoter/related-party exposure** instead. A bank hiding stress shows it in *rising SMA-2 and restructured
loans and falling PCR* — not in a Beneish index.

## 9. Risks (to the constructive view)

1. **Asset-quality normalisation faster than benign** — an unsecured-retail/MFI slippage cycle (the usual
   starting point) turning credit costs up from a cyclical trough.
2. **Prolonged NIM compression** if deposit costs stay sticky while the RBI keeps cutting.
3. **Deposit-growth shortfall** — the binding constraint; a funding squeeze caps loan growth for all.
4. **Macro shock** — crude spike → INR/inflation → hawkish RBI → growth and asset-quality drag.
5. **Valuation** — the "clean sector" is consensus; multiples for quality names leave little margin of
   safety (Kotak, AU).
6. **CBDC/deposit disintermediation** — low-probability, high-impact, long-tail (§6).

## 10. Key monitorables / catalysts (next 2–4 quarters)

- Quarterly **NIM trajectory** (trough confirmation) and **deposit-cost** trend.
- **Slippage ratio & SMA-1/2 book** in unsecured retail and MFI — the leading indicator.
- **Credit-deposit ratio** and deposit-growth pace; FCNR(B) inflow follow-through.
- **RBI MPC** (stance shift; further cut vs hold on the 5.1% CPI print).
- **PSU privatisation** signalling; **BoB** re-rating toward book.
- **Wholesale CBDC / tokenised-settlement** pilots scaling (structural, slow).
